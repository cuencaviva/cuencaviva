Music Academy

Making music with the community.

Music Together is an early childhood program designed to develop basic musical skills in young children, such as moving to the rhythm, singing in tune and enjoying music as a natural form of expression. It was created in 1987 by the Center for Music & Young Children in Princeton, New Jersey (USA), and today has more than 300 centres worldwide.

Thanks to the collaboration of Paloma González, we have been able to bring this program to our communities, holding sessions in the schools of Mallín Grande and Puerto Guadal, as well as in community spaces open to families. These sessions have been deeply meaningful: children take an active part through singing, movement and play, developing their ability to listen, coordinate, express themselves and share music together.

This work also presents a real challenge. So far we have only been able to count on Paloma González, who travels all the way from Santiago to lead these sessions periodically. Her contribution has been invaluable, yet the distance and lack of continuity show an urgent need: a consistent musical presence in our schools.

We believe music should be a stable part of children's education and daily life. Through music they develop not only rhythmic and listening skills, but also confidence, emotional expression, memory, teamwork and a sense of well-being. We want our students to experience this every week, with music as a universal language that nurtures creativity, collaboration and all-round development.

That is why our next step is to strengthen this area and seek support to fund a local music teacher or facilitator every week in the rural schools of the basin. In Puerto Guadal and its surroundings we have talented young people, musically skilled, sensitive and community-minded, who could continue this work and help it grow.

Music has the power to unite us, to heal, and to open doors to learning and joy. That is why we want to keep supporting this initiative and make music a living, permanent presence in our classrooms and our community.
